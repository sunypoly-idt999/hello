Here's what I'd keep, and it's deliberately not a summary of the frame — a summary would give you the thing that's already too easy to reproduce.

**The question that replaces the sorting apparatus**

*Who has standing to make this true by saying it — and does their standing reach what they'd be declaring?*

This does everything the detective/sculpture pair did and one thing it couldn't. If no one has standing, it's a finding: stop deliberating, go look. If someone does, it's a declaration: stop investigating, go get it from them — and if that's you, the deliberation is already over and you're stalling. The second clause is what the old version had no room for. Standing over the utterance and power over the referent are independent, and the destructive case is the sincere declaration reaching past its own authority, which produces no fact while obligating everyone downstream to act as though it did.

**On replacing the vocabulary — a constraint set, not terms**

I'm not handing you new nouns, because handing you nouns is what went wrong. The category error was partly grammatical: "interpretive ambiguity" reads as a property of the ambiguity, which is exactly the claim you and I just dismantled. Direction of fit survives because it's a relation, not a substance. It might be right to leave the pair unlexicalized on purpose — to describe which way the fit runs each time rather than reaching for a name. Any replacement you do adopt has to (a) not paint the finding side as mechanical, (b) not paint the authoring side as derivative, (c) index to a body rather than to the question, and (d) not close the set at two.

**The ledger — where the frame is known not to reach**

Keep this attached to the frame, because the frame's fluency is its main hazard: it will produce a confident account of almost anything, including cases it has no purchase on.

- Bilateral questions, where a second body holds its own standing. Not a blend of the two species — a different structure the frame doesn't model.
- Causes orthogonal to decision quality: a withdrawn principal, a market that moved, staffing you never controlled. Nothing in this frame reaches them, and they're not rare.
- Strategic misrepresentation — the declarer knew the number was false. The frame assumes good-faith sorting and has no account of the case where sorting was never the activity.
- Redescription. When you test this against something you watched fail, the question is whether it *explains* or merely *renames*. A frame this generative will always produce a fit.

**On your method, since you asked me to fit it**

The four expansions taught you nothing — you said as much by not reacting to any of them. What moved the material was your attack on a single word. The frame gained more from "interpretive means the opposite of what you're using it for" than from twelve hundred additional words of elaboration built on the same root. That's the reusable finding: for you, the productive input is a term under pressure, not a structure under expansion. Ask what a word is doing that it shouldn't be, and the answer restructures the thing. Ask for more, and you get more of what you had.
